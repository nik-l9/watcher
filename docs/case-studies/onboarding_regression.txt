ANSWER
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Signups genuinely fell the week of 15 July 2026: mobile conversion rate dropped from 3.50% to 2.90% (-17.4%) while desktop conversion rate stayed flat, and onboarding completions fell from an 82–87/day baseline to 63–67/day starting exactly on 15 July.  [ga4.compare_periods 296fc01a, posthog.event_trend 62cb4db0]
  The most likely cause is commit 91c3e4a, PR #913, 'Rework mobile onboarding modal,' merged and deployed to prod-web on 14 July, which shipped with a known, reviewer-flagged defect making the continue button unreachable on small mobile viewports; desktop was explicitly unaffected by this change.  [github.commits b3f2a15f, github.deployment_history 598fd01b, github.pull_request_activity f12caf1f]
  A general-demand explanation was checked and rejected: desktop lost a similar share of sessions but its conversion rate held steady, whereas mobile's conversion rate itself deteriorated, and no commits were found in the native mobile app repo during this period.  [ga4.compare_periods 296fc01a, github.commits b762be41]

HYPOTHESES TESTED
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  [supported] The mobile onboarding modal rework in PR #913, merged and deployed 14 July, broke the continue-button flow on small mobile viewports and caused the mobile conversion-rate drop starting 15 July.
      Timing lines up (deploy 14 Jul, drop starts 15 Jul), the change is mobile-only by the author's own description, the conversion-rate drop is mobile-only, and a known, unfixed viewport defect that blocks the completion button was flagged in review and merged anyway. No other deploy or repo change in the window touches mobile.
  [contradicted] The overall signup drop is explained mainly by a general decline in traffic/demand across channels, not a funnel-specific problem.
      If demand alone explained it, conversion rate would hold roughly steady on both platforms as sessions fell equally (which is what happened on desktop, -0.09% rate change). Instead mobile's conversion rate itself fell 17.36% even though mobile sessions fell by nearly the same percentage as desktop sessions, pointing to a funnel-level problem specific to mobile rather than a traffic-level one.
  [inconclusive] A marketing-site or pricing change (e.g. the pricing-page copy update on acme/web, commit 77aa21b, 12 July) drove the signup decline.
      This commit predates the 15 July onset by three days and deployed 12 July, two days before the observed effect begins, and it would be expected to affect all traffic, not mobile specifically. Not directly ruled out by a contradicting observation, but the timing and device-specificity make it an unlikely explanation; treated as inconclusive rather than contradicted since no direct test of pricing-page traffic was run.
  [contradicted] A separate code change in the mobile app repo (acme/mobile) caused the drop.
      No commits were found in acme/mobile for the window 8–21 July, ruling out a native app change as the cause; the drop is web-funnel based (GA4 sessionConversionRate on mobile web), not native-app based.
