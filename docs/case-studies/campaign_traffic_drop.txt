HYPOTHESES TESTED
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  [supported] The spring-2026-brand paid campaign ran out of budget and was paused around June 14-15, cutting Paid Search traffic and the signups it drove.
      The Slack pause announcement (Jun 14), the campaign-level session collapse (Jun 15), the channel-level Paid Search collapse (Jun 16-30 window vs prior), and the signup level shift (Jun 15) are all consistent in timing and magnitude, and organic channels were unaffected, ruling out a site-wide issue.
  [contradicted] A product or mobile app code deployment caused the signup drop.
      No commits exist in acme/product or acme/mobile for all of June, so no deploy could have caused a mid-month behavioral change. This was the expected-to-fail alternative and it fails cleanly.
  [contradicted] An organic/SEO decline or broader demand drop caused the signup fall.
      Organic Search sessions and conversions were flat (-0.25%/-0.26%) across the same comparison window, showing no broad demand decline outside the paid channel.

RECOMMENDATIONS
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  P1 Decide whether to replenish and resume the 'spring-2026-brand' paid campaign budget, given it was driving roughly 40% of daily signups before it was paused.
      why: Paid Search conversions fell 74% and overall signups fell 42% starting the day after the campaign pause was announced, with no corresponding drop in organic channels.
      [ga4.compare_periods 9e0f5f71, slack.search_messages 783266f2, posthog.event_trend 8c3bd06f]
  P2 Set up a budget-pacing alert so marketing and leadership are notified before a campaign exhausts its budget mid-month, rather than discovering it after signups have already fallen.
      why: The pause was a budget-exhaustion event announced same-day in Slack, not a planned or gradual wind-down, indicating no pacing safeguard was in place.
      [slack.search_messages 783266f2]
